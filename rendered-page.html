5 reports read as a one-day move; filed under parser by tenant.

Shared parser reads month first

For a day-first tenant, the shared parser reads the date month first. For example, an app report from Harbour Dental says 7 April became 4 July (R-41200); another reverses 4 July and 7 April (R-41201). Fixing this path requires an explicit date contract at the boundary and regression checks for each affected channel. Intake tags are a guess, not the cause.

Booking-v3 prints a month-first hint for day-first tenants

In the form channel, booking-v3 displays MM/DD/YYYY while the form reads the date in the tenant’s day-first order. These users follow the displayed instruction and still get a wrong date. Fix the hint and parsing contract together; retest day-first tenants through booking-v3. This cause must not disappear inside the shared-parser ticket.

Uncertain symptom reports stay visible

Some reports describe a one-day move rather than a clear month/day swap. The component’s footnotes identify how many are filed by tenant. Preserve those reports for follow-up: ask for the input value, timezone, expected date and actual date. Their wording alone does not prove a third root cause, and filing them by tenant is not proof of an individual diagnosis.

When the desk received reports and when it merged them

Receipt time and merge time are separate events. A late merge does not date the original incident. The chart computes both series from the source records.

Triage and knowledge changes proposed

Read the tenant’s locale, form and channel before treating two reports as duplicates. Check the report ID first so overlapping export pages do not inflate counts. A matching subject does not prove a matching cause; a different subject does not prove a different cause.
